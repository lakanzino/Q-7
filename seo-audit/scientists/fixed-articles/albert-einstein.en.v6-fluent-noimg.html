Albert Einstein Biography | Photoelectric Effect to General Relativity

Author: Qpedia Editorial — Reviewed by PhD Theoretical Physics (Sharif) — Published: 2026-09-21 — Updated: 2026-09-21 — About | Contact
Albert Einstein was a patent clerk in Bern who in one year rewrote physics four times. In 1905 he showed light comes in packets, atoms are real, time stretches, and mass is frozen energy. Ten years later he turned gravity from a force into geometry and bent the universe. Those two leaps, from photoelectric effect to general relativity, underlie all modern technology from GPS to solar panels.

Childhood in Ulm and Munich: Compass and Violin

Albert Einstein was born 14 March 1879 in Ulm, Germany, into a secular middle-class Jewish family. His father Hermann Einstein was first a featherbed salesman and later owner of an electrochemical factory, and his mother Pauline Koch ran the household. The family did not strictly observe religious rituals but did not deny its Jewish identity. Albert had a younger sister Maja, born two years later, who remained his closest confidante for life.

Sources: Britannica – birth 14 March 1879 Ulm | Nobel Facts – Einstein 1879-1955

Two childhood memories shaped his path. At age 5 his father gave him a compass and he stared for hours at a needle turning without touch — his first encounter with invisible force. At 12 a small geometry book came into his hands that he called “holy geometry book” and he was fascinated that a few simple axioms could build eternal truths. At same time he played violin and practiced Mozart daily until end of life. At Luitpold Gymnasium in Munich however he was bored by Prussian system; military discipline and rote memorization, something he later said suffocates creativity. A teacher even told him he would never amount to anything.

Source: Britannica – compass violin Luitpold Gymnasium

In 1894 his father's factory in Munich failed and family moved to Milan. Albert was left alone in a Munich boarding house and six months later ran away and reached his parents by train. In 1896 he renounced German citizenship and was stateless for some years until he became Swiss citizen in 1901; a citizenship he kept for life even after becoming American.

Source: Britannica – Milan 1894 stateless 1896 Swiss 1901

Education: From Failure to Doctorate

Einstein failed entrance exam to Zurich Polytechnic in 1895, but his math score was so excellent that head of Aarau school in Switzerland accepted him. He graduated 1896 and entered ETH Zurich, where he met Mileva Marić, Serbian classmate, and Michele Besso, lifelong friend. His professor Hermann Minkowski later translated relativity into geometric language, though at time he saw Einstein as lazy student but with bold ideas. He graduated 1900 as teacher of physics and mathematics and in 1905 received doctorate from University of Zurich with thesis “New determination of molecular dimensions.”

Source: Britannica – ETH 1896-1900 PhD 1905

In 1902 he was hired as expert class III at patent office in Bern. His job was checking electromagnetic inventions, but in his desk drawer he solved equations of light. This quiet job gave him time to ask deepest questions without academic pressure: What would you see if you moved at speed of light? That childhood question later became relativity.

Source: Nobel Biographical – patent office Bern 1902

Miracle Year 1905: Four Papers that Shook World

Year 1905 is called annus mirabilis or miracle year. 26-year-old Einstein published four papers in Annalen der Physik, each founding a branch of physics. It is unprecedented in history of science that one person in one year gives both quantum of light, proof of atoms, relativity of time, and E=mc².

Source: Britannica – miracle year 1905 four papers

1. Photoelectric — Light is Particle — March 1905

First paper 17 March 1905 titled “Heuristic viewpoint on production and transformation of light.” Einstein said light is made of energy packets E=hν, same idea Planck had said for oscillators but Einstein extended it to light itself. This explained why frequency of light matters for knocking electron out of metal, not intensity. Robert Millikan 11 years later verified it precisely and Einstein won Nobel Physics 1921 for this law, awarded 1922. Today same law is basis for solar cells and camera sensor in your phone.

Sources: Einstein 1905a photoelectric DOI | Nobel 1921 photoelectric | Nobel Stories – smartphone camera

2. Brownian Motion — Atoms are Real — May 1905

Second paper 11 May 1905 explained random motion of pollen grain in water by molecular collisions and gave formula for average displacement that made Avogadro number measurable. Jean Perrin in 1908 verified it with microscope and 2000-year doubt about existence of atoms ended. Einstein brought philosophy into laboratory with one equation.

Source: Britannica – Brownian motion 1905 atoms

3. Special Relativity and E=mc² — June & September 1905

Third paper 30 June 1905 “Electrodynamics of moving bodies” had two principles: laws of physics same in all inertial frames and speed of light in vacuum constant for all observers. From these, time dilation and length contraction followed. Fourth paper September 1905 was a corollary: mass and energy equivalent, E=mc². This formula later explained nuclear energy, but Einstein always stressed it is only a geometric result of relativity, not recipe for bomb.

Sources: Einstein 1905c special relativity DOI | Britannica – E=mc2

4. General Relativity — Gravity is Curved Spacetime — 1915

Einstein worked 10 years on generalizing relativity and on 25 Nov 1915 completed field equations: matter tells spacetime how to curve, spacetime tells matter how to move. He predicted light from star passing near Sun bends 1.75 arcseconds. Arthur Eddington during eclipse 29 May 1919 in Africa and Brazil measured this bending and Times London 7 Nov 1919 headlined “Revolution in science — Newton ideas overthrown.” Einstein overnight became most famous scientist in world.

Sources: Britannica – general relativity 1915 Eddington 1919 | Stanford – Einstein field equations 1915

Personal Life, Faith and Ethics

Einstein married Mileva Marić 1903; Lieserl 1902, Hans Albert 1904 and Eduard 1910 were children. Marriage ended divorce 1919 and same year married cousin Elsa Löwenthal until her death 1936. He was known for humor, humility and messiness; messy hair, leather jacket and violin always with him. Friend of Charlie Chaplin and said imagination more important than knowledge.

Source: Britannica – marriages Mileva Elsa

Regarding religion grew up in secular Jewish family. At 12 had brief religious phase but later said “I believe in Spinoza’s God who reveals himself in harmony of world.” In famous letter 3 Jan 1954 to Eric Gutkind wrote “Word God for me is nothing but expression of human weakness.” Called himself agnostic not militant atheist and sentence famous: “Science without religion lame, religion without science blind.”

Source: Stanford – Einstein religion Spinoza Gutkind 1954

Ethically pacifist and signed anti-war manifesto 1914. In 1933 when Hitler came to power was in US and Nazis burned his books calling “Jewish physics.” He resigned from Prussian Academy and moved to Institute for Advanced Study Princeton. On 2 Aug 1939 signed letter written by Leo Szilard warning Roosevelt Germany may build atomic bomb; letter led to Manhattan Project but he himself did not work on it. Later called signing it “one great mistake.” After war advocated nuclear disarmament and civil rights for Black Americans and in 1952 declined presidency of Israel.

Sources: Britannica – pacifism Nazi 1933 Roosevelt 1939 civil rights | Nobel Biographical – Princeton 1933 US citizen 1940

On 18 April 1955 died of ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm in Princeton. He refused surgery saying “I want to go when I want, it is tasteless to prolong life artificially.” Until last hours worked on unified field theory.

Source: Britannica – death 18 April 1955 aneurysm

Legacy in Your Pocket

Without special relativity, GPS satellite clock would lose 7 microseconds per day and without general relativity gain 45 microseconds; without both, navigator after one day would have kilometers error. Without photoelectric no camera and solar panel and Einstein’s 1917 laser equation is basis for fiber optics. Gravitational waves LIGO saw 2015 are same spacetime ripples Einstein predicted 1915.

Source: Britannica – GPS relativity LIGO waves

Common Myths

1. Failed school: No, genius in math.

2. Built atomic bomb: No, did not work on Manhattan, only warning letter.

3. Nobel for relativity: No, for photoelectric, relativity still controversial.

4. Militant atheist: No, agnostic Spinozist.

Einstein's style of work was unique. He imagined thought experiments — riding a light beam, falling in an elevator, twins traveling at light speed — and turned them into equations. He worked with little formal mathematics at first, relying on physical intuition, then learned the necessary geometry from friends like Marcel Grossmann. He was not a lone genius in vacuum; he corresponded with Planck, Bohr, Schrödinger, Born, and many others, sometimes agreeing, often debating fiercely, especially about quantum probability. This combination of imagination, persistence, and dialogue is what made his 1905 and 1915 breakthroughs possible.

Sources: Britannica – thought experiments, Stanford – Grossmann collaboration

Conclusion

Albert Einstein born 14 March 1879 Ulm, shook world with four papers 1905, made gravity geometry 1915, won Nobel 1921 for photoelectric and died 18 April 1955 Princeton. His E=mc² and photon still in your pocket, from GPS to solar panels.

FAQ

Who was Albert Einstein?
German-Swiss-American physicist who 1905 gave photoelectric and special relativity and 1915 general relativity, Nobel 1921.
Why did he win Nobel?
For discovery of law of photoelectric effect E=hν, not relativity.
Did he build atomic bomb?
No, did not work on Manhattan, only signed warning letter to Roosevelt 1939.
Was he religious?
Secular Jewish agnostic Spinozist, 1954 Gutkind letter says God is expression of human weakness.
What is legacy today?
GPS with relativity, solar panel and camera with photoelectric, LIGO with general relativity.
